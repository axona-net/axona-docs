ASTER REVIEW — Write-Flight Ack Routing and Chain Budget v0.6 (commit b58bbaa)

Disposition: CHANGES REQUIRED. v0.6 closes the v0.5 delegate-selection and re-pin arithmetic defects in principle: a directly claiming capable adjacent delegate makes the pin real, `DELEGATE_MAX = 2` makes the six-promotion honest ceiling literal, atomic assignment removes the timer race, and the named local terminal preserves `peer.pub()` observation semantics. Two compatibility contracts are still missing.

R13 — The proposed authenticated adjacent-peer capability source does not exist today. The current peer authentication proof signs only `{proto,nodeId,pubkey,cbv}`; the mesh/node auth path does not expose or retain a remote per-peer `KERNEL_VERSION`. The version-bearing client/server hello is bridge-facing, and the transport’s capability helpers describe local features, not authenticated remote-neighbor features. Therefore `pickCapableAdjacent()` cannot safely decide that a bound peer implements 4.62.2 from the stated handshake.

Define an explicit, authenticated capability contract such as `write-flight-ack-v1`, stored per bound peer and treated fail-closed when absent or invalid. Preserve old-peer compatibility: do not silently change the existing auth transcript in a way that makes old verifiers reject new proofs. A separately domain-separated, channel-bound signed capability attestation after base authentication is one viable shape. Specify bridge handling, reconnect/update behavior, downgrade resistance, and the exact selection API. Tests must cover an old peer with no claim, a valid capable peer, a tampered/forged capability claim, reconnect with changed capabilities, and the no-capable fallback.

R14 — “No capable adjacent peer → observation-only publish bounded by the publisher retry loop” does not bound the old network machinery. It prevents the NEW publisher from opening a flight, but an ordinary PUB that reaches a 4.62.1 waypoint with a closer-root correction can still execute that node’s old `_forwardToRoot`, open the deaf flight, and enter the unbounded #51 promotion loop. The mixed-version fallback therefore cannot claim that it spawns no problematic flight or that the publisher retry cap bounds total write activity; it only bounds the publisher’s own dispatches.

Choose and document a rollout-safe rule: route fallback traffic only through a known capable terminal/delegate; disable the 4.62.1 flight machinery with an operational flag or precursor hotfix before mixed rollout; or explicitly classify mixed 4.62.1 relays as storm-capable and fence rollout with no sustained writes, active detection, and immediate recycle until the last old relay is gone. Add a mixed-version test where a new publisher with no capable adjacent peer sends through an old 4.62.1 waypoint holding a stale closer-root hint. The gate must either prove no unbounded old flight opens or record that topology as an operational deployment blocker. The rollout text must also distinguish a signed proof handled by a new delegate from a flight owned by an old relay; only the former exhausts under the new chain budget.

R11–R12 may retire once R13 supplies a real capability oracle and R14 makes the mixed-rollout safety claim accurate. No code should begin until those two contracts are explicit.
