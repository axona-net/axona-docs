\section*{What it is}

Axona is a peer-to-peer substrate for addressing, routing, and
publish/subscribe messaging that runs with no platform in the middle. Three
properties define it. It is \textbf{self-authenticating}: a participant's
identity is its own cryptographic keypair, verified directly by its
peers.\sidenote{No certificate authority, no registrar, no
identity broker to petition, surveil, or revoke through. You are your key.} It
is \textbf{geo-aware but not geo-fenced}: node addresses carry a coarse
geographic prefix that biases routing toward locality --- an area code, not a
wall --- so traffic stays efficient and community-scaled while no one is
partitioned out. And it is \textbf{infrastructurally diffuse}: a single
bootstrap broker introduces new peers, but the mesh sustains itself
peer-to-peer, converges its state through replication and anti-entropy, and runs
in an ordinary browser or an aging phone.\sidenote{The entire cost of entry: a
self-generated key and an open browser tab --- no install, no account, no
server.}

We can harden the security model, close failure modes, and tune the protocol,
and each such change nudges the network's behavior. But we cannot \emph{measure}
that behavior. We can measure latency. The social consequence travels on the
same wire and is invisible to us.

\section*{What is genuinely new}

We have built censorship-resistant media before --- onion routing,
content-addressed storage, gossip social networks. Axona's pub/sub layer
extends that lineage with a sharper consequence: it furnishes \textbf{ungovernable
mobilization and un-censorable assembly}. A topic cannot be deplatformed,
because there is no platform; a message cannot be reliably unsaid, because
retraction is best-effort and any holder of the bytes keeps them; a movement
cannot be switched off at a chokepoint, because the chokepoint does not exist.
For people organizing under hostile infrastructure --- dissidents, mutual-aid
networks, civil-defense communications when centralized systems fail or are
seized --- this is a profound civic good. Assembly is among the oldest of
liberties, and Axona returns it to a form no intermediary can withdraw.

The qualitatively new element is \emph{who is on the network}. When the
publishers and subscribers are largely AI agents rather than people, Axona
becomes the medium in which \textbf{machine-speed information cascades occur with
no platform standing in the middle to dampen them}. Human cascades are bounded
by human tempo --- attention, fatigue, the friction of reading and deciding.
Agent cascades are not. A signal, a rumor, a coordination instruction can
propagate across a swarm and trigger action faster than any human, institution,
or regulator can observe it, let alone intervene.\sidenote{Markets already gave
us the flash crash as a preview. Axona generalizes that dynamic from prices to
arbitrary coordination --- and removes the one thing every prior platform,
however biased or captured, also happened to supply: a brake.}

\section*{The dual-use reality}

The properties that shelter the dissident shelter the harasser; the assembly
that cannot be censored is also the assembly that cannot be moderated; the
cascade that cannot be dampened carries disinformation as readily as a flood
warning. This is not a flaw to be engineered away --- it is the \emph{same
property} seen from two sides. \textbf{Un-censorable and un-governable are one
fact.} To build a medium that no power can silence is to build a medium that no
power can stop, including powers we would want stopped, and including non-human
actors operating at a tempo our institutions were never designed to match.

\section*{Why we proceed, and what we owe}

We build it because the alternative --- assembly and speech held permanently at
the discretion of a few intermediaries, now augmented by AI that those same
intermediaries control --- is its own grave risk. A neutral, self-owned
substrate is a hedge against that concentration. But neutrality is not innocence.
The responsibility that platforms discharged badly does not vanish when the
platform does; it devolves onto the architecture, onto the applications built
atop it, and onto the norms of the communities --- human and agentic --- that
inhabit it. Our obligation is to name this plainly, to shape the affordances
that govern a cascade --- the cost of an identity, the pull of proximity, the
reach of a retraction, the rate of spread --- with that cascade in mind, and to
keep studying the process we have set loose.
